In July 2025, one month before building [this site](/en/), I did the classic exercise with ChatGPT: define my personal brand. Full questionnaire — mission, values, passions — then generation of a signature line, an editorial strategy, and a sample article.

Eleven months later, the site exists, and so does the brandbook. I pulled up the original document and ran the diff.

## What ChatGPT had produced

A signature line: **"Decoding the world around us to better understand, create, and share."**

An editorial strategy in four pillars (AI, methodology, teaching, posture). An article template with the instruction to "always end with an opening — a question, a possible application, or a doubt deliberately left hanging." And a complete sample article, structured with 🔹 and 👉 emojis, that opened with:

> Sharing an idea isn't just passing it along. It's making it understandable, actionable, and sometimes memorable.

At the time, I was pleased. It looked like personal branding. That was exactly the problem.

## What survived: all of the substance

The questionnaire held up. Eleven months on:

- **The mission "Share. Teach. Discover"** became the first line of my manifesto: "I design learning experiences and I build them."
- **The three core values** it surfaced (knowledge, innovation, competition/risk) became the site's three activities: instructional design, AI consulting, game studio.
- **Being a multipotentialite**, which I described back then as something to justify, became the site's very architecture. I no longer explain it — I display it.

Why did it hold? Because ChatGPT didn't produce it. The substance came from my answers to the questionnaire. The AI structured; it invented nothing.

## What died: all of the style

The signature line was never used. Anywhere. Not one page, not one bio, not one post. It was replaced by **"Intent before the tool"** — four words found while writing the manifesto, not while brainstorming taglines.

As for tone, the 2025 sample article now violates just about every rule in my Voice & tone section:

| 2025 (ChatGPT)                      | 2026 (brandbook)              |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| "Always end with an opening"        | No cliffhangers               |
| "A doubt deliberately left hanging" | Subject + verb + proof        |
| Structural emojis 🔹👉✍️            | Zero decorative emoji         |
| "AI as a cognitive partner"         | Show the work, skip the story |

## The honest read

ChatGPT did the collection work well and the writing work poorly. Which makes sense. The substance came from me: a well-run questionnaire is a free coaching session. The style came from the average of 2025 LinkedIn posts — and writing like the average is the opposite of a brand.

The other lesson: a voice isn't generated, it's subtracted. It took me eleven months of [publishing](/en/blog/creating-educational-content/) to know what I don't write. No model could have given me that in July 2025, because it didn't exist yet.

If you do this exercise with an AI: keep the questionnaire answers, throw away the prose.

The 2025 template recommended to "always end with an opening." This one ends with a period.
